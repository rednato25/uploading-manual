## Who this is for

Anyone making a 1080p encode from a 1080p Blu-ray remux.

## When you use this

When the 1080p x264 slot is free on the tracker and you have a good remux. Read [Encoding basics](/encode/encoding-basics/) first.

## Before you start

- [StaxRip](https://github.com/staxrip/staxrip) installed
- A remux that passed [Finding a good remux](/remux/finding-a-good-remux/)
- The tracker's encode rules from the tracker page
- Your preset and CRF, picked with a [test encode](/encode/test-and-compare/), or a bitrate from the calculator

<details class="ui-reference">
<summary>What each tune changes (reference)</summary>

A tune changes a few settings on top of the preset. Values are from the x264 source (`common/base.c`) and `x264 --fullhelp`. Without a tune, x264 uses the defaults in the first row.

| Tune | Deblock | psy-rd | aq-strength | qcomp | Other changes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| none (defaults) | 0:0 | 1.0:0.0 | 1.0 | 0.6 | ipratio 1.4, pbratio 1.3, deadzone-inter 21, deadzone-intra 11 |
| `film` | -1:-1 | 1.0:0.15 | 1.0 | 0.6 | none |
| `grain` | -2:-2 | 1.0:0.25 | 0.5 | 0.8 | `--no-dct-decimate`, deadzone-inter 6, deadzone-intra 6, ipratio 1.1, pbratio 1.1 |
| `animation` | 1:1 | 0.4:0.0 | 0.6 | 0.6 | 2 more B-frames, reference frames doubled (1 if it was 1) |
| `stillimage` | -3:-3 | 2.0:0.7 | 1.2 | 0.6 | none |
| `psnr` | 0:0 | off | 1.0 | 0.6 | `--aq-mode 0` (AQ off), `--no-psy` |
| `ssim` | 0:0 | off | 1.0 | 0.6 | `--aq-mode 2`, `--no-psy` |
| `fastdecode` | off | 1.0:0.0 | 1.0 | 0.6 | `--no-cabac`, `--no-deblock`, `--no-weightb`, `--weightp 0` |
| `zerolatency` | 0:0 | 1.0:0.0 | 1.0 | 0.6 | `--bframes 0`, `--rc-lookahead 0`, `--sync-lookahead 0`, `--sliced-threads`, `--no-mbtree`, `--force-cfr` |

What the settings do:

- **Deblock**: smooths block edges. Negative values smooth less and keep more fine detail.
- **psy-rd**: two numbers. The first keeps detail and sharpness the eye notices. The second (psy-trellis) keeps fine texture such as grain.
- **aq-strength**: how much AQ moves bits between flat and detailed areas of a frame.
- **qcomp**: how much quality may drop in fast, complex scenes. Higher keeps quality more even across scenes, at a higher bitrate.
- **ipratio, pbratio**: how much better I-frames are than P-frames, and P-frames than B-frames. `grain` keeps them closer.
- **deadzone, dct-decimate**: both throw away small details to save bits. `grain` turns them down so the grain stays.

Only one of `film`, `grain`, `animation`, `stillimage`, `psnr` and `ssim` can be used at a time. `fastdecode` and `zerolatency` are for playback on weak devices and live streaming, not for releases.

</details>

## AQ mode

AQ (adaptive quantization) moves bits inside each frame, toward flat areas such as skies and walls. x264 uses mode 1 by default.

x264 describes mode 3 as "Auto-variance AQ with bias to dark scenes" (`x264 --fullhelp`). If your film has a lot of dark scenes, try `--aq-mode 3` on a [test encode](/encode/test-and-compare/) next to one with the default. Keep it only if the frames look better.

## Reference frames and level 4.1

Reference frames are earlier frames the encoder can copy from. More need more decoder memory, so each level has a cap. At level 4.1 the cap depends on the frame size:

| Resolution | Max reference frames at level 4.1 |
| --- | --- |
| 1920x1080 | 4 |
| 1920x800 | 5 |

The x264 program handles this in two ways (`x264.c`, `encoder/set.c`):

- **`--ref` not set**: x264 lowers the reference frames to fit the level. Preset slow asks for 5 and slower asks for 8; at 1920x1080 both become 4.
- **`--ref` set**: x264 keeps your value even if it breaks the level, and only prints a warning (`DPB size ... > level limit`). The file then claims level 4.1 but needs more memory than a 4.1 player has.

So leave `--ref` unset, or set it no higher than the table. After the encode, check `ref=` in the settings line in MediaInfo.

## Filters

<GoodBad>
  <p slot="good">Only Source and Crop are on.</p>
  <p slot="bad">StaxRip can switch filters on for you. Depending on the profile, StaxRip may start with a deinterlace filter (Field, for example QTGMC) and a denoise filter (Noise, for example DFTTest). On a progressive Blu-ray, deinterlacing damages the picture and denoising removes the grain. Turn both off.</p>
</GoodBad>

## Steps

<Steps>

1. **Load the remux.** Drag it into StaxRip.

2. **Pick x264** from the video encoder menu and open its options.

   <Shot
     name="encode/06-staxrip-x264-options.png"
     alt="StaxRip x264 options window with rate control, preset, tune, profile and level"
     caption="The StaxRip x264 options. Set CRF or two pass, preset, tune, profile and level here."
   />

3. **Set rate control.** Choose CRF and type your value, or choose two pass and type the bitrate in kbps.

4. **Set preset, tune, profile and level** as in the table.

5. **Check the filters.** Open the filter list. Only Source and Crop should be on.

6. **Crop.** Run auto crop, then step through a dark and a bright scene in the preview. Fix by hand if a bar is left or picture is cut.

   <Shot
     name="encode/07-staxrip-crop-filter-list.png"
     alt="StaxRip filter list with only Source and Crop enabled"
     caption="The StaxRip filter list. Only Source and Crop should be enabled."
   />

7. **Set the audio.** Follow the tracker's audio rule. Copy (mux) the track as it is, or convert it as required. Write down the final audio format, because the file name must match it.

8. **Set subtitles.** Keep the tracks the tracker requires, with languages set.

9. **Set the target file name** with the release name. If the audio is FLAC 2.0, the name says `FLAC.2.0`, not `AAC.2.0`.

10. **Start the encode.** A 2-hour film at preset slow takes about an hour per pass on a modern 12-core CPU.

11. **Check the encode** with the [checking guide](/encode/checking-your-encode/) before uploading.

</Steps>

## Stop and ask someone if

- Auto crop gives an odd number, or very different results in different scenes.
- The film switches between aspect ratios (some IMAX films do).
- The source is interlaced or 25 fps when the film should be 23.976.
