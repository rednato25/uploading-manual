## The torrent row

| Column | What it counts | What it tells you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Size** | Total size of the files | How much counts against your download (unless it is freeleech). |
| **Seeders** (S) | People connected right now who have the whole file and are uploading it | More seeders means a faster download. Zero seeders means nobody can give you the file. |
| **Leechers** (L) | People connected right now who are still downloading | They are who you upload to. Many leechers and few seeders is where you earn upload fastest. |
| **Snatches** or **Completed** (C) | How many times the torrent was fully downloaded, ever | Shows how popular it was over time. A high number with few seeders means people downloaded and left. |
| **Age** | Time since upload | New torrents have the most leechers. |
| **Peers** | Seeders plus leechers | Total people connected. |

<Aside title="Reading health at a glance">
  Lots of leechers and few seeders: high demand, and a good torrent to seed if you already have the file. Lots of seeders and no leechers: easy to download, but you will upload very little from it.
</Aside>

## Your account numbers

| Number | Formula or meaning | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Uploaded** | Total data you sent to others | The thing you build up. |
| **Downloaded** | Total data you received (freeleech does not count) | The thing that costs you. |
| **Ratio** | Uploaded ÷ Downloaded | Most trackers have a minimum, often somewhere between 0.4 and 1.0. Below it you get a warning and then lose download rights. Check the exact number on each tracker. |
| **Buffer** | Uploaded minus Downloaded | How much you can download before your ratio drops below 1.0. A 50 GB buffer means you can grab 50 GB without going under. |
| **Seed time** | How long you kept seeding a torrent | Many trackers require a minimum per torrent. |
| **Seed size** | Total size of everything you seed right now | Some trackers reward this with bonus points. |
| **Bonus points** (BP, seedbonus) | Points earned per hour of seeding | Usually spent on upload credit, freeleech tokens or invites. Large and poorly seeded torrents tend to earn more. |

### A worked example

You downloaded 200 GB and uploaded 150 GB.

- Ratio = 150 ÷ 200 = 0.75
- Buffer = 150 − 200 = −50 GB

You are above a 0.6 minimum but below 1.0. If you now download a 40 GB remux that is not freeleech, your ratio becomes 150 ÷ 240 = 0.63. One more large download and you hit the minimum.

## Torrent tags

| Tag | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Freeleech** (FL) | The download does not count. The upload still does. The cheapest way to build ratio. |
| **50% freeleech** | Only half the size counts against you. |
| **Double upload** (2x) | Your upload on this torrent counts twice. |
| **Internal** | Made by the tracker's own release group. |
| **Personal release** | The uploader made it themselves, not copied from elsewhere. |

## Hit and run (HnR)

A hit and run is downloading a torrent and stopping before you seeded it for the required time or ratio. Each tracker sets its own rule, for example "seed for 7 days or reach 1.0 on that torrent". Too many open HnRs and the account gets restricted.

To avoid a hit and run, never delete a torrent from your client the day it finishes. Leave it seeding until the rule is met, or longer.

## Uploading earns more than downloading

When you upload a new release, you are the only seeder. Every leecher downloads from you, so a popular upload can earn many times its own size in the first days.
